# Fact-check report B (brd, cnd, flo mitigation, six mitigation sections, glossary/FAQ, DOMS)

Checked 2026-09-25. Statutes were read on flsenate.gov in their 2026 version. Where a guide cites an older year, the 2026 text was compared against it. ES and EN were read side by side for every item below. Unless a row says otherwise, ES and EN say the same thing.

Verdict key: VERIFIED = the cited primary source supports the claim (or, for a claim with no footnote, a primary source I checked supports it). "VERIFIED (unsourced)" = a general market-practice statement that has no footnote and that no primary source states outright. It is plausible and matches the related guide text. These are listed together at the end so you can decide whether to keep them.

Access notes: fema.gov, naic.org and travel.state.gov block curl. FEMA pages were read with WebFetch. The FEMA PDFs were downloaded through WebFetch and their text extracted with pypdf. NAIC and State Department pages were read from same-year Wayback Machine copies. Every source URL listed below resolved (HTTP 200) through at least one of these routes.

## Trap results

1. **Fla. Stat. 617.0834 (2026).** Rewritten by s. 52, ch. 2026-168 (CS/HB 797, 2026). Last action 6/26/2026; **effective July 1, 2026**. Chapter 617 is renamed the "Florida Nonprofit Corporation Act". The 2026 text now covers any director, and any officer serving without compensation, of a corporation under the chapter. The 2025 text was limited to 501(c)(3), (4) and (6) organizations. The 2026 text also adds a distinct standard for derivative and member suits (conscious disregard or willful or intentional misconduct). It keeps the criminal-law, improper personal benefit and third-party recklessness, bad-faith and wanton-and-willful exceptions. The guide's wording and its "revised in 2026" note are accurate.
2. **Volunteer Protection Act (42 U.S.C. 14503).** The conduct standard and "no effect on the organization's liability" are accurate. The guide omits the licensing condition and the vehicle exception, so the sentence is CORRECTED.
3. **Fla. Stat. 718.111.** (1)(d) points to 617.0834. (11)(h) requires insurance or fidelity bonding of all persons who control or disburse funds, covering the maximum funds in custody. (11)(f)1 requires the master policy to cover property as originally installed or like kind and quality. (11)(f)3 has the unit-owner exclusion list, which the guide reproduces accurately. (11)(j) makes deductibles and excess damages a common expense. The 2026 text changes only cross-references (s. 42, ch. 2026-14; s. 189, ch. 2026-168). brd cites the 2025 URL, which is accurate but should move to 2026.
4. **Fla. Stat. 627.714.** At least $2,000 of loss assessment coverage for all assessments from the same direct loss, with a deductible of no more than $250 per direct property loss, and no deductible if one was already applied to the same loss. (4) requires the excess clause. Unchanged since ch. 2021-99; all VERIFIED.
5. **Fla. Stat. 553.899 (milestone inspections).** The current text is identical in 2025 and 2026 and was last amended by **s. 4, ch. 2025-175 (HB 913), effective July 1, 2025**. That act added "habitable", made the local repair-timeline ordinance mandatory, and added bid disclosure and annual reporting. There was **no 2026 change**: SB 722 (2026) died in Regulated Industries on 3/13/2026. The following are all VERIFIED: three habitable stories, Dec 31 of the 30th year, every 10 years, the local 25-year option (salt water), phase one visual by a licensed architect or engineer, phase two only on substantial structural deterioration (may include testing), and 45-day distribution of the summary plus posting.
6. **Fla. Stat. 718.112(2)(g) SIRS.** The 2026 text changes only cross-references (s. 43, ch. 2026-14). The substantive changes came in **ch. 2025-175, effective July 1, 2025**:
   - "habitable" added;
   - (g)9, which lets an association delay a SIRS for up to 2 budget years after a milestone inspection;
   - (f)2.e, which allows a majority-vote temporary pause or reduction of reserve contributions for up to 2 budgets, for budgets adopted on or before Dec 31, 2028, when a milestone inspection was done in the previous 2 years.

   Still in force: every 10 years; three habitable stories; the listed items (roof; structure; fireproofing and fire protection; plumbing; electrical; waterproofing and exterior painting; windows and exterior doors; other items over $25,000); Dec 31, 2025, or with the milestone inspection, but "in no event" after Dec 31, 2026; and no vote to waive or reduce reserves for (g) items in budgets adopted on or after Dec 31, 2024, except a multicondominium with a Division-approved alternative funding method. The guide omits the 2025 pause exception, so the summary line and the reserve paragraph are CORRECTED.
7. **Hurricane deductible "often a percentage of the building's value" (no footnote).** Unsupported, and it describes what insurers typically set. 718.111(11)(c) says the board sets deductibles consistent with industry standards and local prevailing practice, based on available funds. CORRECTED with statutory wording.
8. **FEMA zones, BFE, freeboard.** All nine zone definitions, BFE and freeboard match FEMA. Source [14] does not display V, VE or D, so the source is CORRECTED.
9. **Risk Rating 2.0.** Oct 1, 2021 for new policies, Apr 1, 2022 for renewals, building-specific rate, most increases capped at 18%. All VERIFIED against the July 2025 fact sheet.
10. **CRS.** 5% steps up to 45% for Class 1. Applies to all Regular Program NFIP policies in the community, including outside the SFHA. VERIFIED. The page says "Last updated September 24, 2026", which matches the label.
11. **Retrofitting (P-312 ch. 3 and the "Protect Your Home" brochure).** Elevation, flood vents, wet floodproofing, dry floodproofing (including the basement and SD/SI cautions), backflow valves and barriers are VERIFIED. The utilities line is CORRECTED: "at least 1 foot" does not cover water heaters.
